How To Set Up A Google Business Profile

You set up a Google Business Profile by creating the listing, entering accurate details, and verifying ownership, all free through Google. Here is how.

By Sayan Roy Chowdhury · 2026-07-23 · 5 min read

How do you set up a Google Business Profile?

You set up a Google Business Profile by creating the listing, entering your business details, and verifying that you own the business, all free, through Google. The whole process takes a single session for most owners, though verification can take a few days to complete. Once it is live, your business can appear on Google Maps and in local search results.

The setup itself is simple. The part people rush, and regret, is accuracy. Every detail you enter now becomes a signal Google uses to rank and describe you, so it pays to get each one right the first time.

What do you need before you start?

A few basics, gathered in advance, make the process smooth.

  • A Google account, ideally one you control long term, not a staff member's personal login.
  • Your exact business name, as it appears on your signage.
  • Your address or service area, depending on whether customers visit you.
  • Your phone number and website, matching what you use elsewhere.
  • Your hours, including any seasonal or holiday variations.
  • A few good photos, of the place, the team, and the work.

Having these ready means you fill the profile in one clean pass rather than leaving gaps you forget to return to.

What are the setup steps?

  1. Go to the Google Business Profile site and sign in with your chosen account.
  2. Enter your business name. If it already exists, claim it instead of creating a duplicate.
  3. Choose your primary category carefully, since it strongly shapes which searches you appear for.
  4. Add your location or service area, exactly as it should read everywhere.
  5. Enter contact details, your phone and website, matching your other listings.
  6. Set your hours, accurately, including holidays.
  7. Start verification, usually by postcard, phone, email, or video, depending on what Google offers you.

How does verification work?

Verification proves you actually run the business, so Google can trust your listing. The most common method is a postcard with a code sent to your address, which you enter to confirm. Some businesses are offered phone, email, or video verification instead. Until you verify, your edits may not show publicly, so treat this step as essential rather than optional.

If a postcard does not arrive, you can request another. Do not create a second listing in the meantime, since duplicates confuse Google and split your signals.

What should you do right after setup?

Finish the profile completely before you celebrate. Add your services with plain descriptions, write a genuine business summary, upload your photos, and publish a first Google Post. A profile that is verified but half-empty ranks poorly, because Google reads blank fields as a less active, less trustworthy business.

This is also the moment to make your facts agree everywhere. Your name, address, and phone on the profile should match your website and every other listing, exactly.

What mistakes should you avoid?

A handful cause most of the trouble. Stuffing keywords into your business name, which breaks Google's rules and risks suspension. Choosing a vague primary category. Using a phone number or address that differs from your website. Leaving fields blank. Creating a duplicate when one already exists. Each of these quietly weakens the listing before it has a chance to rank.

How do you keep the profile healthy after setup?

Setup is the start, not the finish. A profile left untouched slowly loses ground to rivals who tend theirs, so build a light routine from the beginning. Each week, reply to any new reviews and add a short post. Each month, glance over your hours, services, and details to catch anything outdated. Whenever something real changes, new hours, a new service, a renovation, update it the same day. None of this takes long, and the habit compounds over time. A profile that is set up carefully and then maintained steadily becomes one of the most reliable sources of local customers a small business has, and it all runs on a free listing.

What happens once it is set up well?

A complete, verified, accurate profile starts working quickly for local searches. It can appear in Maps, feed the Map Pack, and even inform the AI assistants that now answer local questions. The setup is only the foundation, the ranking comes from reviews, activity, and consistency over time, but a profile built carefully from day one gives all of that the strongest possible start. Rush the setup and you spend months fixing it. Do it properly once and everything afterward gets easier.

Key takeaways

  • Setup is free and quick, but accuracy is what makes the profile work, so get every detail right the first time.
  • Verification proves you run the business; until you verify, edits may not show publicly.
  • Avoid keyword stuffing the name, vague categories, mismatched details, blank fields, and duplicate listings.
