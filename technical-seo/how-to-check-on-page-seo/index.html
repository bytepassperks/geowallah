How To Check On Page SEO

Checking on-page SEO means working through each page's elements systematically to find the gaps. Here is a practical method and the free tools to use.

By Sayan Roy Chowdhury · 2026-09-27 · 5 min read

How do you check on-page SEO?

You check on-page SEO by working through each page's key elements, its title, headings, content, meta description, URL, images, and internal links, and asking whether each is clear, useful, and matched to what people search. The check finds the gaps between what a page could be and what it is, so you know exactly what to improve. It needs no expensive tools, just a method and an honest eye. This guide walks through how to check a page properly.

The value of a check is in being systematic, since the easiest problems to miss are the obvious ones you stop noticing on your own pages.

What elements should you check?

The on-page elements that shape how a page ranks and reads.

  • Title tag, clear, honest, and matched to the search.
  • Headings, a single sensible H1 and logical subheadings.
  • Content, genuinely useful and answering the real query.
  • Meta description, an inviting, accurate summary.
  • URL, short and readable.
  • Images, with descriptive alt text.
  • Internal links, connecting to related pages.

Going through these in order turns a vague sense that a page underperforms into a specific list of what to fix.

How do you check the content itself?

By reading it as a visitor would, not as its author. Ask whether the page actually answers the question someone typing the target search has in mind, whether it covers the topic thoroughly, and whether anyone would find it genuinely useful. This is harder than checking a title, because you wrote the content and know what you meant, so you read past its gaps.

A useful trick is to imagine a specific person with a specific question landing on the page. Would they get their answer, or leave to find it elsewhere? That honest test reveals more than any tool, because it measures the one thing that ultimately decides rankings: whether the page genuinely satisfies the person who came for it.

What tools help you check?

Free tools cover most of the work. Google Search Console shows which searches a page already ranks for, revealing whether it matches the intent you assumed. PageSpeed Insights flags speed problems that undercut even good content. A browser's view-source or developer tools let you confirm titles, headings, and alt text are actually present and correct.

None of this requires a paid subscription. The tools surface the technical facts; your judgement supplies the harder question of whether the content truly serves the reader.

Does a thorough check actually improve rankings?

It does, because the fixes it surfaces are often the ones holding a page back, and sound on-page work shows in results. Emily's Pet Heaven in Barrackpore reached the top of its Map Pack and is named by ChatGPT and Gemini partly because its pages are clear and well-matched, exactly what a good on-page check confirms, alongside a complete profile and real reviews.

The check itself changes nothing. Its worth is in directing your effort to the specific weaknesses that, once fixed, let a deserving page finally rank.

How do you act on what the check finds?

A sensible order, from substance to detail.

  1. Fix content gaps first, since content matters most.
  2. Correct the title to match the real search intent.
  3. Sort the headings into a clear, logical structure.
  4. Rewrite a weak meta description to earn the click.
  5. Tidy the URL and alt text where needed.
  6. Add internal links to and from related pages.

Working from content outward means you fix the things that matter most first, rather than polishing small details on a page whose substance still falls short.

Is checking on-page SEO worth the effort?

For any page you want to rank, yes, because the check is free, quick, and almost always finds something worth fixing. Pages drift over time, intent shifts, content dates, small errors creep in, and a periodic check catches what you would otherwise miss. The work is entirely in your control, so there is nothing stopping you acting on what you find. Picture two pages on the same topic: one is checked and tidied every few months, the other published once and forgotten. Only the first stays sharp and keeps ranking. Checking on-page SEO is worth the effort because it turns a vague worry that a page underperforms into a clear, fixable list.

Key takeaways

  • Check title, headings, content, meta, URL, images, and internal links systematically.
  • The hardest check is reading your own content as a visitor with the question, not as its author.
  • Free tools surface the technical facts; your judgement decides whether content truly serves the reader.
