Local SEO Audit

A local SEO audit is a structured check of everything that decides whether nearby customers find you. Here is what to check and how to run one.

By Sayan Roy Chowdhury · 2026-09-01 · 5 min read

What is a local SEO audit?

A local SEO audit is a structured check of everything that decides whether nearby customers find you, so you can see exactly what is holding your rankings back. It looks at your Google Business Profile, your facts across the web, your reviews, your website, and your listings, then turns the findings into a short list of fixes. The point is not a long report. The point is knowing what to do next.

Most businesses guess at why they are not ranking. An audit replaces the guessing with a clear picture of the gaps.

Why should you run an audit first?

Because fixing the wrong thing wastes months. Without an audit, owners often pour effort into a new website while a duplicate listing or inconsistent phone number quietly holds them down. The audit finds the real bottleneck, so your effort lands where it counts.

It also gives you a baseline. Once you know where you stand today, you can measure whether your work is actually moving the needle, rather than hoping it is.

What does the audit check on your profile?

Start with the Google Business Profile, since it drives the map results.

  • Verification status, since unverified profiles often do not show.
  • Category accuracy, because the primary category strongly shapes your searches.
  • Completeness, every field filled, hours correct, services listed.
  • Photos and posts, checking the profile looks active rather than abandoned.
  • Suspensions or duplicates, which can hide or weaken your listing.

A profile that fails any of these is leaking visibility before the rest of your work even begins.

What does it check across the web?

Your facts, your reviews, and your reputation. The audit confirms that your name, address, and phone match everywhere they appear, since inconsistency makes Google hedge. It reviews how many honest reviews you have, how recent they are, and whether you reply. It looks at your citations on relevant directories, and at the local links and mentions that build prominence.

This part often surfaces the quiet killers: an old address on a directory you forgot, a second listing nobody claimed, reviews left unanswered for a year.

What does it check on your website?

Whether your site backs up your profile. The audit looks for clear location and service pages, contact details that match your listing exactly, fast loading on a phone, and content that genuinely helps a local customer. It also checks the basics that any search relies on: a site that works on mobile, loads quickly, and is easy for Google to read.

A strong website does not replace the profile, but a weak one drags on everything above it.

How do you run a simple audit yourself?

  1. Search your business name and category, from your real area, and note where you appear.
  2. Open your profile dashboard and check verification, category, and completeness.
  3. Check your facts on your website and top directories for any mismatch.
  4. Review your reviews, their number, recency, and whether you reply.
  5. Test your website on a phone for speed and clarity.
  6. Write down the gaps and rank them by likely impact.

How often should you audit?

Once is never enough. Your facts drift, competitors move, reviews age, and Google changes, so a local presence that was clean six months ago can quietly slip without you noticing. A light quarterly audit suits most small businesses: a quick pass over the profile, the facts, the reviews, and the website, with a deeper look once a year. The aim is to catch small problems before they cost you rankings. Treat the audit as a regular health check rather than an emergency response, and you spend your time maintaining a strong position instead of rescuing a weak one. The businesses that audit steadily rarely face the nasty surprises that catch the ones who never look.

What do you do with the findings?

Fix the foundation first. A useful audit produces a prioritised list, not a pile of tasks, so tackle the issues that block visibility, verification, duplicates, inconsistent facts, before the cosmetic ones. Then re-check in a few weeks to see what moved. This is the same disciplined loop GEOwallah uses for local clients: audit, fix the weakest signal, measure, repeat. An audit you act on, steadily, is worth more than a flawless report that sits in a drawer. The businesses that climb are simply the ones that turn their findings into fixes, one at a time, while rivals keep guessing.

Key takeaways

  • An audit replaces guesswork with a clear picture of what is actually limiting your local visibility.
  • Check the profile first, then your facts and reviews across the web, then your website.
  • Act on the findings in priority order, fixing foundation issues before cosmetic ones, then re-check.
