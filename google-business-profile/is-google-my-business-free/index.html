✦ Google Business Profile, Reviews & Map Pack Ranking

Is Google My Business Free

Google My Business, now Google Business Profile, is completely free to create, verify, and use. Here is what is free and what genuinely costs money.

By Sayan Roy Chowdhury · 2026-06-29 · 5 min read

Is Google My Business free?

Yes. Google My Business, now called Google Business Profile, is completely free to create, verify, and use. There is no listing fee, no monthly charge, and no paid tier you must reach to appear in the map results. Google offers it free because every accurate listing makes Google Maps and Search more useful, which keeps people coming back.

That surprises a lot of owners, partly because so many emails and calls imply you must pay to "activate" or "secure" your listing. You do not. If someone is charging you a fee simply to keep your profile live, you are being misled.

What exactly is free?

Almost everything that matters.

  • Creating and verifying your listing.
  • Every profile field, including name, category, hours, services, and description.
  • Photos and Google Posts, with no limit that a normal business would hit.
  • Receiving and replying to reviews.
  • Messaging, calls, and direction requests from customers.
  • Performance insights, showing how people find and act on your listing.

None of these sit behind a paywall. The features that decide whether you rank in the Map Pack are exactly the ones Google gives away for nothing.

So what does cost money?

Two things, and neither is the profile itself. The first is advertising. Google Ads can place you above the free results, but that is a separate, optional product, not a requirement for the profile to work. The second is help. You might pay a person or an agency to set the profile up well and keep it optimised, which is paying for time and skill, not for the listing.

Knowing this difference protects your wallet. The profile is free. Doing it brilliantly, or paying someone to, is where any real cost lives.

Why do scams around this exist?

Because confusion is profitable. Some companies cold-call businesses claiming the listing will be "removed" unless a fee is paid, or that they are "Google" offering verification for a charge. Google does not work that way. It will never demand payment to keep your free profile online.

Treat any such message with suspicion. Check directly inside your own Google Business Profile account, and ignore invoices from third parties you never hired.

If it is free, why doesn't everyone rank?

Because free does not mean effortless. The listing costs nothing, but the results come from doing the unglamorous work, completing every field, keeping facts consistent, earning honest reviews, and staying active. Plenty of businesses claim a profile, add a phone number, and stop. They have a free listing that does very little.

This is the quiet opportunity. Since the tool is free and most rivals barely use it, the business that fills it out properly and tends it weekly can climb past competitors who never bothered.

How do you make the most of the free profile?

  1. Claim and verify it, so you fully control the listing.
  2. Complete every field, leaving nothing blank.
  3. Match your facts across your website and other directories.
  4. Add real photos and refresh them regularly.
  5. Post and reply to reviews to keep the profile active.
  6. Check insights to see which searches bring you customers.

Should you pay for ads instead?

Ads and the free profile are not rivals, they are different tools. Google Ads can place you above the free results instantly, which suits a brand-new business with no ranking yet or a short promotional push. But ads stop the moment you stop paying, while a well-tended free profile keeps working month after month at no cost. The sensible order for most local businesses is to get the free profile right first, since it is the durable foundation, and to consider ads only as a deliberate top-up for a specific goal, not as a substitute for the work the free listing already rewards. Spend on ads if it suits you, but never because you think the profile itself demands payment.

What is the real takeaway?

The profile is free, valuable, and underused, all at once. You should never pay Google to have one, and you should never let a scam convince you otherwise. Take a Sodepur restaurant that simply filled in its free profile honestly and kept it current: it began surfacing for nearby food searches without spending a rupee on the listing. That is the lesson in miniature. The cost of ranking locally is rarely money. It is the attention most businesses never give to a tool that already sits there, free and waiting.

Key takeaways

  • The Google Business Profile and every ranking-relevant feature are free; you should never pay Google to keep it live.
  • The only real costs are optional: advertising and paying someone for the time and skill to optimise it.
  • Free does not mean effortless, which is why a properly tended profile beats the many that are claimed and abandoned.
