✦ AI Search Optimization: GEO, AEO & LLM Citation

LLM SEO

LLM SEO is the practice of making your business easy for large language models to understand, trust, and repeat. Here is how it works.

By Sayan Roy Chowdhury · 2026-09-17 · 4 min read

What is LLM SEO?

LLM SEO is the practice of making your business easy for large language models to understand, trust, and repeat. The large language models behind ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity answer questions in plain prose. LLM SEO is how you make sure that when one of them answers a question in your category, your name is in the reply rather than a competitor's.

The phrase is new. The core idea is old and simple. Be clear, be consistent, be trustworthy, so a system that summarises the web summarises you accurately. Get that right and the models become a quiet, steady source of customers who arrive already convinced. Get it wrong and they recommend the business next door without ever mentioning you.

How does this differ from classic SEO?

Classic SEO optimises for a crawler that ranks pages. LLM SEO optimises for a model that reads, compresses, and rephrases them.

A crawler can reward a page stuffed with the right keywords. A model does the opposite. It strips your wording down to the underlying facts, so vague filler and keyword padding simply vanish in the summary. What survives is the clear claim, the concrete detail, the fact stated plainly enough to travel from one page into an answer. That flips the old playbook on its head. You win by being precise and quotable, not by being dense and stuffed with terms. A short, true sentence beats a long, padded paragraph every single time, and it always will, because a model rewards clarity over volume.

What makes a model trust your business?

Models are cautious by design, and caution rewards consistency.

  • One set of facts. Your name, address, phone, and category should match across the web with no drift.
  • Plain, self-contained claims. Sentences a model can lift without needing the rest of the page to make sense.
  • Corroboration. When several trusted sources describe you the same way, the model's confidence rises.
  • Freshness. Current dates and updated facts signal that you are still here and still open.
  • Structure. Schema markup labels your content so a model reads it correctly instead of guessing.

Which tools actually help?

A few free habits beat any paid shortcut. The most useful "tool" is the model itself: ask ChatGPT and Gemini your key questions and study who they name and why. Google Search Console shows the real questions people bring to you. A schema validator confirms your markup is clean. Paid suites like Ahrefs or Semrush help with the underlying search work that feeds the models. Treat any product marketed as a one-click fix with healthy suspicion, because this is groundwork, not a button.

How do you get started?

  1. Audit your facts. Make every profile agree on the basics before anything else.
  2. Rewrite for clarity. Replace filler with plain claims a model can quote.
  3. Add schema. Mark up your business, articles, and FAQs.
  4. Earn mentions. Get described accurately on the sites and directories that matter in your field.
  5. Measure the answer. Query the models monthly and track whether your name appears.

Where is this heading next?

Toward more models, not fewer. Today it is ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity. Tomorrow it is the assistant baked into your phone, your browser, and your maps app. Each one answers in prose and names a handful of businesses. The habits that make you quotable now, clean facts, plain claims, real corroboration, carry forward to every new model, because they are not tricks tied to one system. They are simply what trust looks like to a machine. Build them once and you stay ready as the list of assistants keeps growing.

What happens if you ignore this work?

You slowly become invisible to the fastest-growing way people search. A customer asks an assistant, hears a confident recommendation, and acts on it. If that recommendation is never you, the loss leaves no trace in your analytics, because the customer never visited a page you could track. The businesses investing in clarity and consistency now are quietly teaching the models to recommend them. The ones waiting are teaching the models to recommend someone else. Picture a Barrackpore clinic that fixed its facts early and a rival that did not. A year on, only one of them is the answer.

Key takeaways

  • Models compress content to its underlying facts, so vague filler and keyword padding vanish in the summary.
  • Trust comes from consistent facts, plain self-contained claims, corroboration, freshness, and clean schema.
  • The best LLM SEO tool is the model itself: query it monthly to see who it names and why.
