Local SEO & Citations

Local SEO is everything you do to get your business found by customers near you. Here are the core parts and how to start ranking locally.

By Sayan Roy Chowdhury · 2026-06-15 · 5 min read

What is local SEO?

Local SEO is everything you do to get your business found by customers near you, when they search for what you offer. It covers your Google Business Profile, your reviews, your website, and the listings that mention you across the web. The goal is simple: when someone nearby searches for your service, you are the business they see, trust, and choose.

It differs from ordinary SEO in one important way. Ordinary SEO chases rankings across the whole web. Local SEO chases the customer down the road, the one ready to call or visit today.

Why does local SEO matter?

Because local intent converts. Someone searching "vet near me" or "best pet boarding in Barrackpore" is not idly browsing. They want a nearby option, and they usually act fast, often within the day. Winning that moment is some of the highest-value marketing a small business can do.

It also levels the field. A small, well-run business can outrank a large chain on local searches, because proximity and trust matter more than budget. The corner shop that does local SEO properly can beat the national brand that ignores it, simply because Google is trying to serve the nearest, most trusted answer rather than the biggest name.

What are the core parts?

Think of it as a few connected pieces, each reinforcing the others.

  • Your Google Business Profile, the free listing that drives the map results.
  • Reviews, which prove you are real, active, and worth choosing.
  • Citations, your name, address, and phone listed consistently across the web.
  • Your website, which confirms your location, services, and expertise.
  • Local links and mentions, which build your reputation in Google's eyes.

When these agree with one another, Google trusts the picture and ranks you. When they conflict, a clearer competitor wins instead.

How does Google decide local rankings?

It weighs three things: relevance, distance, and prominence. Relevance is how well you match the search, shaped by your profile and content. Distance is how close you are to the searcher, which you cannot change. Prominence is how well known and trusted you appear, built from reviews, links, and consistent facts.

You cannot move your premises, but you can win decisively on relevance and prominence. Done well, that is often enough to beat a closer business with a weaker, neglected presence.

Today they sit first in the Barrackpore Map Pack for "best pet boarding," hold a 5.0 rating, and stay open 24 hours. Ask ChatGPT or Gemini the same question and they name Emily's too. Bookings now arrive straight from search, with no advertising. That is local SEO doing its job.

How do you get started?

  1. Claim and complete your profile. Fill every field accurately and verify ownership.
  2. Fix your facts. Make your name, address, and phone identical everywhere online.
  3. Earn reviews. Ask happy customers regularly, and reply to every review.
  4. Build citations. List your business on the directories that matter in your area.
  5. Strengthen your website. Add clear location and service pages that match your profile.
  6. Stay active and measure. Post regularly and track your rankings for key searches.

Does local SEO still matter with AI search?

More than ever, and here is why. AI assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini lean on the same signals Google uses, your consistent facts, your reviews, your reputation. A business with strong local SEO is the one these tools name when someone asks for a recommendation nearby. So the work is no longer just about Maps. It is about being the answer wherever the question is asked.

What does ignoring it cost you?

The customers who never find you. While you wait, a competitor completes their profile, gathers reviews, and becomes the default choice in your area, on Google and in the AI tools alike. You rarely see the loss, because the customer simply chooses the business above you and never knows you exist. The fix is rarely money. It is steady attention to a set of free signals that decide, every single day, who gets chosen in your neighbourhood.

Key takeaways

  • Local SEO targets high-intent nearby customers ready to call or visit, where proximity and trust beat budget.
  • Its core parts, profile, reviews, citations, website, and links, must all agree for Google to rank you.
  • The same signals make AI assistants name you, so local SEO now decides answers on Maps and in ChatGPT alike.
